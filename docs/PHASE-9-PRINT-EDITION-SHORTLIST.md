# Phase 9 — Print edition shortlist

**Internal. Not published.** No prints are offered on the site, no prices exist, and no supplier has
been chosen. This is a starting point for a decision that is yours.

## How these were chosen

Drawn from the 23 photographs already carrying an editorial role in the published edit — frames that
have survived several curation passes. Weighted for wall readability (does it hold at a distance),
resolution, subject strength, and rights simplicity.

**Rights simplicity matters more for prints than for the website.** A print is a product sold to a
consumer. A frame whose main subject is a recognisable individual raises questions that a landscape
does not, so those are flagged rather than recommended for a first edition.

## Before anything is published

1. **Pick your own twelve.** This list is an opinion, not a decision.
2. **Order test prints** of three or four candidates. Screen strength and paper strength are not the
   same thing. Frame 036 especially — check it at your intended maximum size.
3. **Choose paper and lab.** Matte cotton rag suits the monochrome work; the La Réunion landscapes
   may want more punch.
4. **Decide the edition model.** Open, or numbered and limited. Numbered is more collectable and more
   admin. Do not invent scarcity you will not honour.
5. **Set real prices**, including framing, packing, postage and losses.
6. **Confirm consumer-law obligations** — withdrawal rights, delivery terms, VAT.

## Rights note

Selling a print transfers a physical object, not copyright. The buyer may hang it, resell that copy,
and photograph it in their home. They may not reproduce, license, or commercially exploit the image.
This distinction belongs in the print terms when they are written, and must be written with proper
advice rather than copied from another shop.
