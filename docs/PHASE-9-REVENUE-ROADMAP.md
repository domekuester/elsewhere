# Phase 9 — Revenue roadmap

Channels ranked on the dimensions that actually decide what to do first. No revenue forecasts: there
is no traffic history or conversion data to base one on, and an invented number would be worse than
none.

## Comparison

| Channel | Setup effort | Ongoing effort | Traffic dependency | Margin | Brand fit | Legal complexity | Speed to first revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Studio** | Done | High per project | None | Very high | Excellent | Low (contract) | Weeks |
| **Licensing** | Done | Very low | Low | Very high | Excellent | Medium (releases) | Weeks–months |
| **Prints** | High | Medium (fulfilment) | Medium | Medium–high | Excellent | Medium (consumer law) | Months |
| **Brand collaborations** | Medium | High | Medium | High | Good | Medium | Months |

| **Advertising** | Low | Low | **Extreme** | Very low | **Bad** | Medium (consent) | Not viable yet |

## Ranking

**1 — Fastest to first revenue: Studio.** Independent of traffic. One commission from one hotel
exceeds what advertising would produce in a year at this scale. The page is live, the enquiry path
works, and the site itself is the portfolio. The bottleneck is outreach, not software.

**2 — Lowest maintenance: Licensing.** The photographs already exist. A licence is an email, an
invoice, and a file transfer. Marginal cost near zero. The only real work is the rights review that
turns `ENQUIRY_ONLY` frames into confirmed states — and that only needs doing for frames people
actually ask about.

**3 — Best long-term upside: Studio → Editions.** Studio compounds through referral: hospitality
businesses talk to each other, and each commission produces new portfolio work. Editions layer on
top, converting archive depth into a second income from work already made.

**4 — Best brand fit: Licensing and Editions.** Both treat the photograph as the product. Both are
what a photographic publication is *supposed* to earn from. Neither requires the site to behave
differently, host anything ugly, or interrupt a reader.

## What not to do, and why

**Advertising.** At realistic early traffic this earns single-digit euros per month while degrading
the one thing the site has: uninterrupted photography. It is not implemented and should stay that
way until traffic makes the trade-off obviously worth it — which is far beyond the 90-day horizon.

**Stock syndication.** Ranked last on brand fit for a reason: it competes with your own licensing at
a fraction of the price, and hands your best frames to a marketplace that commoditises them. If it
is ever used, it must be a *deliberately separate* selection — never the frames that carry the
publication. See [PHASE-9-STOCK-SYNDICATION-STRATEGY.md](PHASE-9-STOCK-SYNDICATION-STRATEGY.md).

**Affiliate before traffic.** Commission on near-zero traffic is near-zero revenue, and the temptation
to write for the link instead of from experience is exactly how a publication becomes a content farm.
The architecture exists so it can be switched on later, correctly, once Field Notes bring real
search traffic.

## Sequence

**Now (0–30 days)** — Studio and Licensing live. Domain, Search Console, image indexing. Outreach
begins.

**Next (30–60 days)** — Deepen one destination. Publish the first genuine Field Notes. Rights-review
the frames that get asked about. Select the print shortlist.

**Then (60–90 days)** — Measure which enquiry paths produce real conversations. Test print interest
against actual demand. Only add an affiliate link inside a Field Note that would have been written
anyway.

**Not yet** — Advertising. Stock platforms. Anything requiring inventory or fulfilment before demand
is proven.
