# Phase 9 — Studio outreach system

A repeatable way to approach a small number of the right businesses. **Strategy only — nothing has
been sent, and no contact list has been built or scraped.**

## The principle

Ten researched approaches beat a hundred scraped ones. Every message should be impossible to send to
anyone else, because it refers to something specific you actually noticed about their place.

Volume is the enemy here. A photographer who sends bulk email is indistinguishable from every agency
that also sends bulk email. A photographer who sends five messages that could only have been written
about that one hotel is not.

## Who to approach

**Best fit:**
- Boutique hotels, guesthouses, small design-led places
- Restaurants and cafés with a genuine point of view
- Small tour operators, dive schools, retreat spaces
- Creative hospitality: bakeries, wine bars, roasteries

**The qualifying signal:** *the place is better than its photographs.* A beautiful hotel with a
website full of dim phone snapshots and stock interiors is the entire target market. If their
photography is already excellent, move on.

**Avoid:** chains, anything with a marketing department, anywhere you would not genuinely want to
photograph.

## How to find them

- Places you have actually stayed or eaten — always start here
- Walking a good neighbourhood and noticing
- Instagram location tags in a city you know, filtered by "good place, bad pictures"
- Design and travel guides, then check their sites

**Not**: scraped directories, bought lists, "hospitality contacts" spreadsheets.

## The offer

Four shapes, in ascending value:

1. **Photography** — a shoot, delivered edited
2. **Visual story** — photography plus a sequenced edit they can actually use
3. **Website** — an image-led site around their existing photography
4. **Story & Site** — new photography, the edit, and the site as one commission

Lead with what they visibly need. A hotel with decent photographs and a broken website needs (3). A
hotel with a fine site and weak photographs needs (1). Most need (4), but do not open with the
biggest number.

## The message

Short. Specific. No attachments. One link.

**Structure:**
1. One line naming something concrete about their place — proves you have been or looked properly
2. One line on what you noticed about their photography, without insulting it
3. One line on what you do, with the link
4. One question, easy to answer

**Rules:**
- Under 150 words
- No "I hope this email finds you well"
- No design critique — nobody commissions the person who told them their site is ugly
- Never attach a PDF
- Link to the site, not a portfolio dump. The site *is* the portfolio and it makes the argument
  better than any deck

**Why the link does the work:** they click, land on the archive or a destination chapter, and see
photography, sequencing and interface quality at once. The proposition demonstrates itself. That is
what the *This site is the portfolio* section on `/studio/` is for.

## Cadence

- **Five messages a week, maximum.** More means less research per message.
- **One follow-up**, seven to ten days later, two lines. Then stop. Permanently.
- Never chase a third time.

## Tracking

A plain text file or a five-column spreadsheet:

```
Place | Where I found them | Sent | Replied | Outcome
```

Do not install a CRM.

## When someone replies

1. Ask what it is for and roughly when — before talking price
2. Ask what they have already (photos, site, brand materials)
3. Propose one shape of work, not a menu
4. Quote a fixed project price, not a day rate
5. Put it in writing before starting: scope, usage rights, delivery, payment terms
6. Take a deposit

## Conversion reality

An expected response rate on genuinely personal, well-targeted outreach is roughly **10–20%**, and
perhaps a quarter of those become conversations. So fifteen messages might produce two or three
conversations and, with luck, one project.

That is a normal, healthy outcome. It also means the first month will look like silence. Silence at
this volume is not failure — it is the sample size.

## What this system refuses to do

- Mass mailing
- Bought or scraped lists
- Fabricated credentials, client names, or testimonials
- Pretending to have visited somewhere you have not
- Free work as a foot in the door
