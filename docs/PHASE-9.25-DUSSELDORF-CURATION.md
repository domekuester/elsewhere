# Phase 9.25 — Düsseldorf curation

## What this body of work is

Before any photograph was placed in a layout, all 22 were reviewed as photography, on contact
sheets and then at full size.

They share one formal idea so consistently that it cannot be accidental: **a hard-edged form cut
against open sky.** A bridge pylon with its cables. A tower shaft. The curve of a pale wall. Church
domes. A glass rotunda. Bare branches. Geese. The camera is almost always tilted up, and the sky is
almost always the second subject.

That is the chapter. Not "Düsseldorf", not a city guide, not a set of landmarks — *looking up in a
place you already know.*

It also gives the chapter a colour signature no other chapter in ELSEWHERE has: hard autumn and
winter teal, saturated blue, and pale cream. Japan is warm density. La Réunion is green and cloud.
Essaouira is ochre and shadow. Düsseldorf is cold sky and geometry.

## The landmark problem, handled

Six of the 22 frames contain the television tower — over a quarter of the archive on one landmark.
Each was judged as a photograph rather than as a subject:

| Frame | Judgement | In chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1310315 shaft rising into teal | Graphic, abstract, the tower as a line | **yes** |
| P1320146 deck with one lit window | A detail, not a monument | **yes** |
| P1320094 head isolated in pink dusk | Strong but a third tower frame would be a habit | no |
| P1320253 tower over a river sunset | Proof of location with lens flare | no |
| P1360018 tower through branches, heavy flare | The flare is the picture, and it is an accident | no |

Two survive, well separated in the sequence. The chapter never opens or closes on the landmark.

## The chapter

**11 frames — hero plus ten.** Deliberately the shortest chapter in ELSEWHERE, because 22
photographs do not support more and padding it would be visible.

Hero: **P1350974** — red cables running down a white bridge pylon into deep blue.

**Rhythm at 1440:** 46 · 38 · 30 · 54 · 38 · 38 · 46 · 38 · 30 · 70. Five distinct widths, quiet
30% beats, and the chapter ends on its largest frame.

**The structural rhyme:** the chapter opens *inside* the bridge — close, hard, saturated — and at
frame 08 looks back at the same bridge from across the water at dusk. Neither frame explains the
other; they simply close a loop.

**The ending:** geese against cloud, full width, bright. The chapter begins in a tree tunnel on the
ground and ends in open sky with nothing man-made in the frame. That is the arc, and it is why the
geese are last rather than merely pretty.

## Two decisions made against a first instinct

**The two plane-tree avenues were split.** The first edit opened with both, side by side. Rendered,
they read as one photograph printed twice — same day, same light, same subject — however different
the perceptual hash said they were. One stayed; the other is archive depth.

**The spruce-needle frame was cut.** It survived two edits as a "breath" between the trees and the
city. Rendered, it sat orphaned in a wide empty band, and it was the only frame in the chapter that
had nothing to do with sky. Removing it tightened the opening and removed the page's worst gap. It
remains public in the archive.

Both were caught only by looking at the rendered page, not the selection.

Likewise, the two pale-curved-wall frames are held apart (positions 04 and 09) and the two tower
frames (03 and 07), so no pair reads as a duplicate.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No Home change.** Düsseldorf earned nothing on Home and asked for nothing. It appears in Home's
  destination list automatically, because Phase 9.2 made that list data-derived.
- **No new CSS for Düsseldorf.** The chapter uses the same sequence grammar as Japan, La Réunion and
  Essaouira. Nothing in the stylesheet names this destination.
- **No Black & White additions.** Measured at zero.
- **No People candidates.** Distant figures on a promenade are not a People photograph, and
  claiming otherwise would weaken a body of work built on 77 frames where a person is the subject.
- **No new visual world.** "Architecture" was not invented; these are `urban`, which already exists.
  Six frames — trees, needles, branches, geese — carry no world at all, which is honest.
- **No copy.** There is no owner narrative for Düsseldorf, so the chapter has none. The provenance
  line states the span and the confirmation source, and the photographs do the rest.

## Archive integration

All 22 are public archive depth at positions 637–658, reachable at
`/archive/?destination=dusseldorf`, and 11 of them form the chapter. As with Japan and La Réunion,
the chapter is driven entirely by `data/destinations.json` and its frames are not separately flagged
`featured` in the archive — Essaouira remains the one Phase 9.1 outlier in that respect.
