made anywhere in the published copy.** Date-level ordering is unambiguous and is all the Story uses.

## Owner facts used

- Japan was a childhood dream, formed by manga, Japanese action series and films, long before he could travel alone.
- 2019: first visit, part of it in Nerima near Tokyo with a friend's mother, then Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka.
- 2025: returned deliberately to get off the standard route, and found work.
- A faint chlorine-like smell in Tokyo is his personal arrival marker. FamilyMart, Lawson, 7-Eleven as recognition.
- A visibly drunk, disrespectful tourist group at a transfer point; his sense that patience with visitors has worn thin in parts of Tokyo; his own self-classification as a gaijin with a camera.
- One day in Tokyo, Asakusa, primarily to photograph.
- Roughly six-hour long-distance bus to Niigata; Japanese motorway service areas genuinely enjoyable — landscape, food, clean toilets, separated smoking areas.
- One night in Niigata, then the ferry.
- About five weeks on Sado at HANAYA: tatami, futon, low table, toilet on the corridor.
- Kotaro and Sami run the house; their daughter Hanako is part of it. Described as a wonderful family.
- Two jobs at once: reception/guests, and tarai-bune operation.
- Shukunegi; tarai-bune, also called hangiri. **Keiji-san was the owner's boss**, not a colleague: the tarai-bune tour operation is his, and he is also one of the few people who still builds the boats by hand (owner-corrected, phase 14.4).
- Daily rhythm: morning pickup, set up the stand, boats to the landing, wait, life jackets, short explanation, tour.
- Learned to steer in a few days, then trusted with guests. Three runnable routes, a fourth too much for him.
- Limited Japanese, "sugoi" as default enthusiasm, translator app as normal practice.
- Keiji-san's German Shepherd Asti — alive during the stay, died later, owner not present, regrets it.
- Extremely clear water; the island friendly on some days and not at all friendly on others in wind and rain.
- Racing bike from Kotaro, used constantly on days off — photographing, eating, moving; ridden out to **the lighthouse and the area around it**. He did **not** cycle to the far north of Sado; northern excursions were not made by bicycle (owner-corrected, phase 14.4).
- **Sado daily routine (owner memory, phase 14.5): the tarai-bune work was physically demanding — it went into the shoulders, back and hands — and he went to an onsen in Ogi very often after work. This was a recurring routine, not a one-off. The supermarket was usually part of the same evening, and he came to enjoy even that.**
- **Onsen identity: UNRESOLVED. Deliberately unnamed in publication — the Story says only "the onsen in Ogi".** Two credible candidates were researched and neither is strong enough, because each is contradicted on one point:
  - *佐渡温泉 御宿おぎの湯 (Sado Onsen Oyado Ogi no Yu)* — a proper day-use onsen facility with a restaurant, which fits a frequent after-work bath. But public sources place it on a 高台 (hilltop) about **five minutes by taxi** from Ogi port, which does not fit the owner's "down the road" memory.
  - *小木温泉 旅館かもめ荘 (Ogi Onsen Ryokan Kamome-so)* — 佐渡市小木町11-7, **ten minutes on foot** from Ogi port and in the town itself, which fits "down the road" and the supermarket area. But its day-use bathing is publicly described as limited (typically weekday mornings, phone ahead), which argues against an easy after-work routine.
  - The nearby supermarket, ジェイエイ・エーコープ佐渡 小木店 (A-Coop Sado Ogi), is at 佐渡市小木町395 — the same town district as Kamome-so, but Japanese block numbering does not establish adjacency, so it does not resolve the question. HANAYA publicly lists both onsen as bathing facilities within roughly 1 km.
  - The supermarket is likewise unnamed in publication: "the supermarket" is the owner's own register and naming a branch would turn an errand into branded travel content.
- Taiko class tried once; karaoke twice with the hangiri team, exactly as imagined.
- Meal invitations happened **a few times**, mostly around arriving and before leaving — not constantly, and he did not "rarely eat alone" (owner-corrected, phase 14.4). The sense of belonging rests on the daily work, the mornings, the boats, the waiting and the karaoke rather than on meals.
- Sado departure by ferry from Ogi: **Kotaro played the flute, Sami waved flags, Hanako drummed** (owner-corrected, phase 14.4 — Sami did not drum). Heavy crying; the tune itself is forgotten.
- Ferry to Naoetsu in Jōetsu; red lighthouse frames are the arrival; then roughly six kilometres on foot to the bus stop with luggage.
- Ordinary suburban streets; children walking home from school alone; one child asked roughly who he was and what he was doing; translator; short conversation.
- Nagoya as transit. Agui, Aichi: Buddhist temple placement, grounds maintenance including mowing, heat felt close to forty degrees, ended early, parted on good terms with no conflict.
- **Renjun is the mother of Shinku, the owner's host at the Agui temple. She is a fashion designer.** She cooked daily — rice, pickled vegetables, sometimes soba, sometimes sushi, once cabbage with minced meat, many dishes whose names he does not know. **They talked directly in English; no translator was needed between them** (all owner-corrected, phase 14.4). Shared exercise class taught by a former Japanese baseball player.
- Temple bell every evening at six, struck with a hanging wooden beam, six strikes, audible across the village.
- No proper photograph of Renjun or of the two of them; he regrets it more than missing photographs of places.
- Jonas, one of his oldest friends, visited; went on to Osaka; owner followed; one very good day and evening.
- Flew back to Thailand. Japan is not finished; wants to go further south, Shikoku high on the list.

## Public / private boundary

- **First names only** for Kotaro, Sami, Hanako, Keiji-san, Renjun, Shinku and Jonas. No surnames, no ages, no schools, no addresses, no family detail beyond that the guesthouse is run by a couple with a daughter, that Shinku hosted the owner at the Agui temple and that Renjun is his mother. Two work facts are published because the owner confirmed them and the Story depends on them: Keiji-san runs the tarai-bune operation, and Renjun is a fashion designer.
- **People are published only where the owner selected the frame.** The 36 archive frames carrying a recognisable person remain `storyCandidate: false` in `data/people-review.json` and the template fails the build rather than render one — which is why the stand, the landing and Keiji-san himself are still absent. Six frames are published, each an explicit owner selection recorded in the same register: two public performances (taiko, dancer), two incidental street crowds (Asakusa, Osaka), companions on a Sado hillside, and Kotaro sculling a tarai-bune.
- **Hanako** is named once, in a list of who lives in the house, and once in the farewell. No image, no age, no other detail.
- **The schoolchild** is described, not quoted verbatim and not photographed. The wording is explicitly approximate ("asked me roughly").
- **Renjun** is named and is the emotional anchor. The absence of her photograph is stated as the owner's own regret rather than filled with a substitute frame, and no external image of her was sought.
- **Tourist criticism** targets behaviour, never nationality. The nationality the owner mentioned is not in any draft, note or published line.
- **Overtourism** is framed as his observation of thinning patience in some places, never as a claim about Japanese people. He places himself inside tourism rather than outside it.

## Fact-sensitive language

- The Tokyo smell is owner perception ("When I land in Tokyo I always notice the same thing"), never an objective claim about the city, and never attached to Sado.
- The northern cape is written as "the north" and captioned as Sado; no cape, rock or lighthouse is named, because no owner confirmation or metadata supports a specific name.
- The child walking home alone is not converted into a claim about Japanese safety. The Story explicitly refuses the generalisation.
- "Magical" does not appear. The word is absent from the published copy by design; the material is left to do it.
- No Japanese dish name was reverse-engineered from a description. "Cabbage with minced meat" stays as the owner said it.
