# How ELSEWHERE makes money

Written for you, not for developers. No jargon. Read this one first.

---

## The short version

ELSEWHERE earns in four ways. They are very different in how fast they pay and how much work they
take.

| | What it is | Realistic first money | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Studio** | People pay you to photograph their place, or to build them a site | Weeks | You do the work, once per client |
| **Licensing** | Someone pays to use a photograph you already took | Weeks to months | Almost none — the photo exists |
| **Prints** | Someone buys a photograph to hang on a wall | Months | Setup once, then packing and posting |
| **Affiliate** | You recommend something you used, get a small cut | Many months | Only worth it with traffic |

**Advertising is not built and should not be.** It pays badly at small traffic and would damage the
thing that makes the site worth visiting.

---

## 1. Studio — your fastest money

**What it sells.** Photography, visual storytelling, and image-led websites. The strongest offer is
the combination: new photographs, an edit that gives them shape, and a site built around them. That
is on the page as **Story & Site**.

**Why it is the fastest.** It does not need traffic. It needs one hotel owner, one restaurant, one
small brand to see the site and think *I want mine to look like that*. A single commission is worth
more than a year of ad revenue at this size.

**How someone contacts you.** They read `/studio/`, click *Discuss a project*, and land on the
contact form with "Studio project" already selected. The message arrives in your email.

**How money actually happens.** You reply, ask what and where and roughly when, quote a price, agree
it in writing, take a deposit, do the work, invoice the rest. There is no pricing on the site
deliberately — you quote per project, and never publish a number you would regret.

**Your part.** Reply quickly. Everything else is already built.

---

## 2. Licensing — your easiest money

**What it sells.** Permission to use a photograph you have already taken. A magazine wants a frame
from Japan. A hotel wants one for their lobby wall and website. You license it. The photograph
already exists, so the work is a conversation and an invoice.

**How it works now.** Someone opens a photograph, clicks *Licensing enquiry* in the corner, and the
form arrives with that exact photograph's number attached. You always know precisely which frame
they mean.

**The important part — and please read this bit.** Owning a photograph is not the same as being
allowed to sell every use of it.

- You own the **copyright** to everything here. That is yours.
- But if a photograph shows a **recognisable person**, a **private building**, an **artwork**, or a
  **brand logo**, those people and owners have rights too.
- For a **magazine article**, that is usually fine — that is "editorial" use.
- For an **advertisement**, it is usually not fine without a signed release from the person.

So the site never says "buy this licence". It says "ask about this photograph", and you answer
honestly per request. Right now **none** of your photographs are marked as cleared for commercial
use, because you have not reviewed them yet. That is the correct, safe starting point.

**Your part.** When an enquiry arrives, ask what it is for. If it is editorial and the frame is
yours alone, say yes and set a fee. If it is an advert and there is a recognisable face in it, say
no unless you can get a release. Never guess.

---

## 3. Prints — later, and only when real

**Not built on purpose.** There is no prints page, because publishing one without real paper, real
sizes, real prices, and a real way to post the thing would be a promise you cannot keep.

**What exists.** A shortlist of the strongest candidates
([PHASE-9-PRINT-EDITION-SHORTLIST.md](PHASE-9-PRINT-EDITION-SHORTLIST.md)), and "Print interest" as
an option on the contact form so that anyone who asks is captured.

**When to build it.** After five or so people have asked. Then you pick a lab, order test prints,
see them in your hands, set prices, and the page gets built around something real.

**One thing to remember.** Selling a print does not sell the copyright. The buyer gets a piece of
paper to hang up. You keep the photograph.

---

## 4. Affiliate — much later

**What it is.** You write about a place you actually stayed, link to it, and if someone books
through that link you get a small commission.

**Why later.** It needs people arriving from Google. That takes published Field Notes, and Field
Notes take real writing about real places.

**The rule built into the site.** A recommendation will not appear unless you have personally marked
it as verified. The site cannot generate recommendations, and anything earning a commission is
labelled as such and carries a disclosure. That protects the one thing that makes a recommendation
worth anything: that it is true.

---

## What to do first

1. Give the site a public email address, so every enquiry path switches on.
2. Choose a domain and launch.
3. Send five personal messages to hotels or restaurants whose photography is poor and whose place is
   good. That is [PHASE-9-STUDIO-OUTREACH-SYSTEM.md](PHASE-9-STUDIO-OUTREACH-SYSTEM.md).
4. Answer whatever arrives.

Steps 1–3 are a weekend. They are also, realistically, where your first money comes from.

---

## The principle

The site does not pressure anyone. There are no popups, no countdowns, no fake scarcity, no "limited
spots". Somebody comes for the photographs, sees that the person who made them can clearly also
photograph, edit and build — and gets in touch.

That only works while the photography stays the point. Every commercial decision here is subordinate
to that.
