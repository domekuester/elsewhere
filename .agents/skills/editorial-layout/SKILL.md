# Editorial Layout

Review the page as publication design rather than component assembly.

1. Inspect the complete rendered sequence at desktop and mobile widths.
2. Mark the opening gesture, pauses, accelerations, transitions, and closing gesture.
3. Judge each image's scale and containment relative to subject, neighboring frames, and text.
4. Identify unintentional repetition in block shape, alignment, scale, or spacing.
5. Propose two or three treatments, then recommend the smallest high-impact one.

Use full bleed, contained images, pairs, asymmetry, and text interruption only when the sequence earns them. Consistency comes from type, spacing logic, color, navigation, editorial principles, and photographic treatment—not identical compositions.

Keep surface distinctions: Destination is a photographic body/place index; Journey is personal narrative and lived sequence; Archive may be denser; People must remain human; B&W needs its own cadence; Home is an entrance, not a feature list.
